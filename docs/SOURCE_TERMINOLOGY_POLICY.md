# Classical Tamil source-terminology policy

## Purpose

Research prose, concept matrices, handovers, and generated analytical views must preserve the terminology of the Classical Tamil source instead of silently replacing it with later social, caste, sectarian, or externally reconstructed identities.

## Required rule

When a source prints a Tamil social, ritual, learned, occupational, political, kinship, or community term, retain that Tamil form in source-level research descriptions.

Examples include source forms such as:

- `அந்தணர்`;
- `பார்ப்பார்`;
- `பார்ப்பனர்`;
- `அரசர்`;
- `வேளாளர்`;
- `பாணர்`;
- and other source-printed terms.

The exact form used by the relevant poem or நூற்பா takes precedence over a generalized label. Do not substitute one Tamil source term for another merely because they may appear related.

## Interpretation boundary

A source-printed social or ritual term proves only that the term occurs in that source context and, where supported, that the source assigns it a role or relationship.

It does **not** by itself establish:

- a later caste identity;
- a later sectarian identity;
- a modern community equivalence;
- an externally reconstructed hierarchy;
- a claim of cultural or religious influence from another textual tradition.

Any such historical claim, if ever researched, must be represented separately as an explicitly classified external-evidence or interpretive assertion with its own provenance. It must never be folded into `SOURCE_EXPLICIT` or `GRAMMATICAL_CONCEPT_EVIDENCE`.

## Tolkāppiyam example

In மரபியல் நூற்பா 71, the source form is `அந்தணர்`; in நூற்பா 72, the source form is `அரசர்`. Research documentation should therefore describe those rules using `அந்தணர்` and `அரசர்` rather than replacing them with later identity labels.

## Frozen-source rule

This policy changes only derived research terminology. It never alters the frozen canonical source text.
